Selected work

Decisions, not features.

Anyone can list the software they've shipped. What actually matters is the call the client had to make — and whether it turned out to be the right one. So that's how these are told: the decision, what I found, and what happened next. Not the technology.

01
Startup · Digital Health

A clinically-governed mental health product, shipped to the App Store.

Miirror Health · eating-disorder recovery, rebuilt lean and HIPAA-compliant by design.

The stakes

Miirror Health builds a digital platform for eating-disorder recovery — the part of care that happens between appointments. That's not a normal app: content that supports one patient's recovery can actively harm another's, so the product needed per-user content safety controls, clinical governance, and HIPAA compliance from day one. The founder had inherited a prototype built by students — over-engineered, unmaintainable, and burning money before launch — and needed an honest read on whether it was worth saving.

What I did

It wasn't worth saving, and I said so. I rebuilt the product deliberately lean, with the compliance designed in rather than bolted on, and with per-user content controls built to the feature set Miirror Health's clinical advisory board defined and approved.

The outcome

Shipped to the App Store for beta, with the clinician dashboard and admin interface behind it. I built all of it, and I still do: the same person who made the product decisions wrote the code, shipped it, and answers for it now that it's live.

Miirror Health — digital-health startup · shipped to the App Store for beta.

02
Theater Nonprofit

The spreadsheets running their whole season were a liability. We replaced them.

Candle House Collective · scattered spreadsheets, rebuilt as one system.

The decision

Candle House Collective runs immersive theater — productions with intricate scheduling, actor assignments, and moving parts. All of it was scattered across a pile of Google Sheets wired together with custom code, and the person who'd built and maintained it had already quit. They brought me in to review what was left and say plainly whether it was safe to keep depending on.

What I found

A real liability. The code holding it together was fragile and undocumented, it broke on them constantly, and the one person who understood it was already gone — so every failure was a scramble with no one able to fix it properly. It wasn't saving them money; it was risk they hadn't priced yet. I recommended replacing it outright.

The outcome

I built a secure web application that automated the processes they'd been doing by hand and pulled all those scattered spreadsheets into one place. They now manage shows, actors, scheduling, and scripts in a single system they can trust — instead of a stack of spreadsheets everyone was afraid to touch.

Candle House Collective — immersive theater company.

03
Nonprofit · Bronx, NY

COVID took their fundraisers overnight. Software kept them open.

Preston Center of Compassion · a raffle app built in a crisis — and a partnership since 2020.

The decision

Preston Center of Compassion serves women, children, and families in the Bronx, funded largely by in-person fundraisers. When COVID-19 hit, those events vanished overnight and the organization was at real risk of closing for good. The question was whether there was any way to raise money without a room full of people.

What I did

The fastest safe path wasn't a big platform or a long build — it was one focused tool that did a single thing well. I built a custom fundraising web app that let local donors buy raffle tickets in real time, so the fundraiser could run entirely online, right away.

The outcome

That first app raised tens of thousands of dollars and kept them in operation through the worst of it. Five years on, I'm still their software person — I build and maintain their fundraising apps, run their email marketing, and manage their summer-camp registration platform and their entire donor CRM.

Preston Center of Compassion — Bronx, NY · client since 2020.
